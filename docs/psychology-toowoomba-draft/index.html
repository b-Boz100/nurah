Anxiety can show up in lots of ways

You might notice racing thoughts, a tight chest or trouble sleeping. You might find yourself avoiding places or people, or feeling on edge without knowing why.

Anxiety is common. It can build slowly over time or arrive after a big change, and it doesn't always have a clear cause.

Talking with a psychologist can help you make sense of what's happening and what might help.

How we can work together

  • Understand what's going on

    Together, we look at what sets off your anxiety, how it affects your day and what you'd like to change.

  • Build skills that fit your life

    Psychologists use approaches like cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT). We work with you on practical strategies you can use at work, at home and in the moments that feel hardest.

  • Go at your own pace

    You choose what we focus on and when. Progress looks different for everyone.

How the Medicare rebate works

  1. See your GP

    Book an appointment with your GP and ask about a Mental Health Treatment Plan. Your GP will check if it's right for you and write a referral.

  2. Up to 10 rebated sessions a year

    Medicare covers up to 10 individual sessions each calendar year. After your first six, your GP checks in on how things are going and can refer you for more.

  3. Claiming made simple

    You pay the session fee on the day. We lodge the Medicare claim for you, and the rebate goes into your bank account. [Confirm Nurah's claiming process, e.g. Tyro Health via splose]

You don't need a referral to see a psychologist. Without one, you pay the full fee and no Medicare rebate applies. Video and phone sessions can be rebated too. [Verify telehealth rebate rules]

Do I need to live in Brisbane?

What's the difference between a psychologist and a counsellor?

Psychologists are registered with the Psychology Board of Australia and trained in assessing and treating mental health concerns. Counsellors offer talk-based support too. If you're not sure which is right for you, give us a call and we'll talk it through.

Is it confidential?

Yes. What you share is kept private. There are a few rare exceptions, like when someone's safety is at risk or the law requires us to share information. Your psychologist will explain these in your first session.

What do I need for a telehealth session?

You'll need a quiet, private space and a phone, tablet or computer. For video sessions, your device will need a camera. Before your first session, we'll send you a link to join and simple instructions.

What if I'm not sure I'm ready?

That's okay. You're welcome to call and ask questions first. There's no pressure to book.
